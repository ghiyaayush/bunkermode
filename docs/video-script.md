## What I changed and why

1. **Fixed "DRPK" → "North Korea"** for clarity. Most viewers won't decode the acronym fast enough. If you want the menacing insider tone, change to "DPRK" — totally valid choice. Both work.
2. **"7 AM in morning" → "7 AM"** — "7 AM" is already morning, the redundancy slows the read.
3. **Added a third hack: KelpDAO.** Three examples is the rhetorical sweet spot, and KelpDAO is the one we replay in the demo, so the viewer hears it before they see it.
4. **Added the demo proof beat (1:32-1:48).** Your original jumps from "what it does" to "they woke up fine". Without showing the product actually working, it sounds like marketing. Eighteen seconds of real Kelp replay is the difference between "claim" and "proof".
5. **Added Module B refusal (1:48-2:05).** The supply chain refusal is the one thing every other agent product won't do. Without it, BunkerMode sounds like every "auto-defender" pitch. With it, it sounds like the only one with judgment.
6. **The "redux" framing on the close (2:05).** Your "they woke up at 8 AM" callback is the strongest beat in the entire script. I just made the time stamp visual ("8 AM" on screen) mirror the opening ("7 AM"). One hour later, same person, different outcome. That's the screenshot.

## POV note

The script moves through three perspectives:
- **0:00-0:18 — I** (victim)
- **0:55-1:48 — We** (founders, response)
- **2:05-2:18 — They** (the protected user)

Each pivot is intentional. It mirrors the actual transformation — from someone who lost everything, to someone who built something so others wouldn't, to the people that something now protects. Don't smooth the transitions. The shifts are the story.

## One question for you

The "I lost $247K" opening — is this literally true, or is it a literary frame? Both are valid:

- **If literal:** the founder authenticity is the strongest opening I've ever seen for a hackathon submission. Sets you apart from every "I built this thing" pitch.
- **If literary:** it's still excellent, but you might want to soften it slightly to "Imagine waking up at 7 AM..." or use a hypothetical name. Otherwise judges and audience may take it as fact and you have to either confirm or correct. That's awkward.

## Reading notes

- The opening three lines (7 AM / wallet empty / $247K gone) are the most important read in the entire video. Slow. Quiet. Like you're admitting something.
- "I'm not the only one. And this isn't the first time." — drop your voice on "first time". Resignation, not announcement.
- The hack history at 0:25 is read FLAT. No emphasis on the big numbers. The numbers are loud enough on their own.
- "What if someone was watching — always — so you didn't have to?" — this is the question your whole product is the answer to. Land it. Don't rush.
- "They woke up at 8 AM" — quiet relief. Almost a smile in the voice.
- "DeFi doesn't sleep. Neither do we." — clean and final. Don't add inflection on "we".
